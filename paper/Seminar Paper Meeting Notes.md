## **Seminar Paper Meeting Notes**

### **Related work**

* Do not spend too much time discussing the results of related work.  
* Focus mainly on how each paper relates to our work, why we selected it, and how it differs from our approach.  
* Consider grouping the papersm using an intuitive theme, such as reward density.

### **Methodology**

* Clearly separate the general methodology from the specific experiments.  
* The methodology should focus on:  
  * the idea of creating a Novice model;  
  * the formalization of the Master/Novice approach;  
  * tensor disentanglement as the proposed way to create the Novice;  
  * how differences between the Master and Novice are calculated.  
* Present the conceptual novelty first, followed by the proposed implementation.  
* The experiments currently focus on one specific feature, but that feature should not be presented as the entire methodology.  
* Everything introduced in the methodology should ideally be supported by experiments. Anything not supported should be moved to the ablations section.

### **Contributions and findings**

* The paper should generally contain three types of contributions:  
  * theoretical or conceptual;  
  * methodological;  
  * empirical findings.  
* Decide how significant each result is. Important results can be presented as core findings, while more limited tests can be framed as ablations.  
* Negative or null findings are also valuable.  
* Be honest and precise when presenting the results.

### **Experiments**

* Mention the models and evaluation suites used.  
* Include figures or visualizations where available.  
* Split experiments into separate sections when there are major conceptual differences between them.  
* Detailed code explanations are not necessary.  
* Every experiment and parameter mentioned should be connected to an explanation.  
* When space is limited, parameters can be stated briefly first and justified later.

### **Results and discussion**

* Keep the results and discussion sections strictly separate.  
* The results section should contain tables, measurements, statistical results, and exact findings.  
* When the null hypothesis cannot be rejected, report the exact statistical measures.  
* The discussion section should interpret the results and explain what they mean for the theory.  
* The overall structure should be:  
  * propose the methodology;  
  * explain the experimental setup;  
  * report the results;  
  * discuss whether the results support the theory.  
* For one or two central hypotheses, the final conclusion can be delayed until all relevant experiments have been presented.

### **Submission and feedback**

* The only graded component is the submission on the 8th.  
* Peer reviews are due on the 25th.  
* We may be able to send the paper to the supervisor early, receive both peer and supervisor feedback, and then revise the manuscript before the final submission.

### **AI-use statement**

Use standard wording explaining that AI was used for limited drafting and coding assistance. Boilerplate or insignificant code may have been generated with an LLM, but everything was reviewed and modified by the authors, who take responsibility for the final work.

Make a genuine effort to write most of the paper ourselves. AI-generated writing is often noticeable, and writing it ourselves makes a significant difference.
